Why Data Readiness
Comes First

⏱ 7 min read ◈ Data & Infrastructure

Most businesses approach AI backwards. They see a tool, they want to use it, and then they discover they don't have the data foundation to actually run it well. Data and infrastructure readiness is not a technical checkbox. It is the difference between AI that works and AI that wastes six months of your time.

Your AIQ score in this domain reflects the current state of how your data is collected, stored, cleaned, and accessed. Low scores here do not mean you cannot adopt AI. They mean you need to build the runway before the plane can take off.

Core Insight

You do not need perfect data to start. You need to understand what data you have, where it lives, and how trustworthy it is. That inventory is your starting point.

The Real Cost of Data Debt

Data debt is the accumulated cost of decisions that made data harder to use. Spreadsheets that only one person understands. Systems that don't talk to each other. Reports that take two days to pull. Every organization has it. The question is whether yours is manageable or whether it will block every AI initiative you attempt.

The good news: you don't need to eliminate data debt before starting with AI. You need to understand it well enough to route around it. Start with the AI use cases that work with what you have, while running a parallel track to build the foundation for the use cases that require more.

The Four
Data Questions

⏱ 8 min read ◈ Data & Infrastructure

Before evaluating any AI use case, your organization needs honest answers to four questions:

  • Where does it live? Data in spreadsheets, siloed apps, and people's heads does not count. Accessible, structured data counts.
  • How fresh is it? Stale data produces stale outputs. Real-time or near-real-time data pipelines are a competitive advantage.
  • How clean is it? Garbage in, garbage out is not a cliche. It is a budget line item for companies that ignore it.
  • Who owns it? Data without ownership is data without accountability. Unclear ownership is one of the most common sources of failed AI projects.

Walk through these four questions for your three most important business processes. The gaps you find are the exact gaps your AI strategy needs to address first.

The Audit Exercise

Pick one high-value data set in your business. Map where it is created, where it is stored, who uses it, and how often it is wrong. That audit takes an afternoon and tells you more than any consultant's slide deck.

Data Ownership Is the Most Overlooked Question

Of the four questions, ownership is the one most businesses skip entirely. They assume someone owns the data because it lives somewhere. But if you asked every person in your organization who is responsible for the accuracy of your customer records, you would get five different answers or none at all. That ambiguity is where AI projects go to die.

Assign owners. Make it explicit. Review the accuracy of each critical data set at least quarterly. This is not glamorous work, but it is the unglamorous work that separates organizations that get AI results from organizations that run pilots that never scale.

Infrastructure &
Use Case Matching

⏱ 8 min read ◈ Data & Infrastructure

Infrastructure for AI does not require a data warehouse the size of a small country. For most small and mid-size businesses, the minimum viable infrastructure looks like this:

  • A single source of truth for your most critical data (even if it starts as a well-maintained spreadsheet)
  • A consistent process for capturing new data without manual intervention where possible
  • Access controls so the right people can reach the right data without a helpdesk ticket
  • A basic audit trail so you know when data changed and who changed it
Practical Note

Cloud-based tools like Airtable, Notion databases, or even Google Sheets with proper structure can serve as your starting infrastructure. The goal is to stop data from living in people's heads and inboxes.

Matching Use Cases to Data Readiness

Every AI use case has a data requirement. The smarter your use case selection, the lighter your infrastructure lift. Here is a practical framework:

  • Low data requirement: Use cases that rely on general knowledge — drafting emails, summarizing documents, answering FAQ-style questions. These work with minimal internal data.
  • Medium data requirement: Use cases that need your company's context — proposal generation, customer segmentation, reporting summaries. These need clean, accessible internal data.
  • High data requirement: Use cases that need real-time or historical pattern recognition — demand forecasting, predictive maintenance, churn modeling. These need serious infrastructure investment.

Start in the low and medium tiers. Build wins. Use those wins to justify the infrastructure investment for the high-tier use cases that actually transform operations.
